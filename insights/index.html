PROPERTY & CONVEYANCING

Buying land in Kenya: begin with verification

Before committing funds to a land transaction, verify the interest being sold, who has authority to sell it and what information the land register reveals.

  • Carry out appropriate official searches and review relevant ownership records.
  • Confirm the seller’s identity and authority to transact.
  • Examine the sale agreement, completion documents and required consents.
  • Seek advice on the transfer, taxes and registration procedure.

Land transactions differ. A title search is an important first step, but further due diligence may be needed.

BUSINESS & CONTRACTS

Commercial contracts: details worth reviewing

An agreement should communicate what each party must do, when obligations arise and what happens if circumstances change.

  • Set out payment obligations, scope and performance timelines.
  • Check warranties, termination provisions and consequences of default.
  • Review the governing law, confidentiality and dispute-resolution terms.
  • Ensure the agreement accurately identifies the parties and their authority.

Suitability and enforceability depend on the facts, the wording and applicable Kenyan law.

FAMILY & SUCCESSION

Wills and succession: understanding the first steps

Planning for your estate or administering the estate of a loved one may involve several documentary and procedural requirements.

  • Identify available wills, asset records and debts.
  • Understand the distinction between a grant, confirmation and distribution.
  • Consider the correct probate or administration application.
  • Obtain individual advice where there are disputes or special circumstances.

Each matter must be considered in light of its facts and the Law of Succession Act.

CIVIL & COMMERCIAL DISPUTES

Resolving a dispute: explore the appropriate route

Not every dispute requires immediate litigation. A considered assessment can help identify negotiation, mediation or court proceedings as available routes.

  • Record the important facts and assemble relevant documents.
  • Check whether a contract provides a dispute-resolution procedure.
  • Identify any applicable notice requirements and limitation periods.
  • Review the risks, costs and practical objectives of possible approaches.

The appropriate forum, process and time limits depend on the particular dispute.
